My thesis looked at short vs long-time existence of homogeneous Ricci flows. I looked at an open problem known as the dynamical Alekseevskii conjecture, which asserts that a homogeneous Ricci flow is immortal if and only if the universal cover of the underlying manifold is diffeomorphic to Euclidean space.

In my project, I was studying this conjecture on the homogeneous spaces $\mathrm{SU}(2) \ltimes \mathbb{R}^{2N+1}/\mathrm{U}(1)$ for $N \geq 3$ (the semidirect product is formed by a real-type irreducible representation of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$) and $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})/\mathrm{U}(1)$. On both spaces, I showed that any Ricci flow has finite-time extinction, confirming the conjecture on these spaces. Additionally, as a consequence of the analysis of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})/\mathrm{U}(1)$, the conjecture is true in dimension five. More details about dimension five and contributions by others to this result can be found in Chapter 4 of the thesis.
